    How DynaSCAN works

    A modular pipeline with a separate trajectory check before execution.

    1. Observe

      Simulated dynamic point observations within a 5 m range.

    2. Track

      Clustering, Kalman filtering, and association across frames.

    3. Predict

      Short-horizon motion from a constant-velocity model.

    4. Plan

      Dynamic-clearance B-splines with left/right trajectory seeds.

    5. Validate

      A two-cylinder body model with turning and translation timing.

    Estimate motion from observations

    The point-observation tracker estimates velocity and maintains identities without reading simulator velocities, object IDs, or future trajectories.

    Check the motion that will execute

    Projected A* supplies static detour references. An independent validator checks optimized trajectories against body geometry and the time needed to turn before moving.

    Preliminary simulation results

    Initial observations from small, controlled kinematic tests. The reported clearance range comes from exact-future diagnostics, not the observation-based tracker above.

    Observed behavior

    Collision-free completion

    Observed in the current controlled scenarios, including choosing the open side when one side is statically blocked.

    Minimum dynamic clearance

    0.217–0.288 m

    Range across six left-blocked runs using exact-future obstacle information. This is a diagnostic result, not a guaranteed safety margin.

    Current scope

    Observation tests use simulator-labeled dynamic points without LiDAR occlusion. Tracking and prediction uncertainty, cluttered scenes, and the gap between nominal turning dynamics and actual execution remain open issues. Reliable dynamic avoidance on hardware has not yet been validated.
